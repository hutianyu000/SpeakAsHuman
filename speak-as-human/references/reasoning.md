# Reasoning

Read relevant sections when the entrypoint and scenario rules leave uncertainty about attribution, scope, intent, or reader interpretation. These details clarify the shared constraints; they do not add a mandatory pass to every expression task.

## Claims and attribution

An attribution must refer to an identifiable speaker, document, or body of evidence and accurately represent what it supplies. A claim appearing in input material does not establish that its author conducted a study, collected statistics, or verified the result. Do not add an evidentiary actor, source, method, or verification status without a basis. Vague references to source material, research, reports, or data must not substitute for missing support. When discussing supplied material, make its referent clear and distinguish what it states from what has been independently checked.

A rebuttal must address an accurately represented position or a relevant alternative with a stated basis. Corrective contrast must carry a real distinction. A familiar debate about the same topic does not establish that the user holds any of its usual positions. Interpret supplied stance, quoted positions, and your inference separately.

Check that each conclusion follows from its support and that definitions, premises, and conditions stay consistent. Related statements do not form an argument merely by appearing together. Do not repair a missing inference by adding a connective or silently assuming a premise. In long text, compare claims and scope across sections; retain distinct cases when their conditions explain an apparent difference.

## Scope and uncertainty

Before deleting a framing phrase or qualifier, check whether it limits the viewpoint, time, place, object, operating conditions, evidence, or strength of the claim. Grammatical fluency after deletion does not establish that the phrase was redundant. Preserve its function or recast it if deletion would broaden the claim or increase certainty. Consolidate repeated expressions of the same uncertainty while retaining distinct conditions and sources of uncertainty.

Check the referent and attachment of quantities and qualifications, not just their continued presence. Moving a phrase can change which entity has an area, limit, result, or property. Do not assume two subjects share a measurement or scope merely because they appear in the same sentence.

Identify the proposition modified by an intensifier or comparison and interpret its strength in its domain context. Retain defined technical meanings, supported comparisons, and calibrated uncertainty. Claims of superiority or significance need their relevant baseline and scope; do not replace absent support with stronger wording.

## Context and agency

For context-dependent wording, identify the proposition it asserts, its basis, and its function for the reader. Judge the asserted meaning rather than a familiar phrase pattern or grammatical subject alone. Preserve necessary contrast, supported emphasis, supplied reactions, and technical descriptions when they satisfy these conditions. These judgments do not relax explicit prohibitions.

Interpret domain-defined roles and descriptions of biological function in context. Established technical shorthand may describe observable behavior or supported mechanisms, but must not imply an unsupported conscious intention or motive. A domain label alone does not establish intention. Recast misleading wording around the supported actor, process, or relationship; do not invent an actor when it is unknown or replace an unsupported motive with an unsupported cause.

Preserve the author's supplied stance or reaction within the task's boundaries. Do not add a personal stance, emotion, or contrarian pose to simulate a human voice. Figurative agency belongs to an authorized creative or figurative task and must not be passed off as a factual explanation.

## Reader interpretation

Use the knowledge reasonably expected of the intended audience, the artifact itself, and references explicitly available to them. Do not rely on private conversation history to supply missing meaning. Check entities, terms, pronouns, sources, conditions, and requested actions. For names and comments, consider a maintainer who did not see the task. Repair missing context within scope without inserting the conversation history.

Describe this as independent reader testing only if a separate reader actually assessed the artifact without that history. The perspective check by the authoring agent is an internal review, not evidence of an independent assessment.
