# Reasoning

Read relevant sections for unresolved attribution, scope, agency, or reader-inference questions.

## Claims and attribution

A claim in the input does not establish that anyone studied, measured, or verified it. Check whether attribution identifies a source and supports both the result and the claimed evidence-gathering activity. Vague references to research, reports, or data cannot supply missing support. Missing evidence in an excerpt does not prove that the source lacks it.

A rebuttal needs an accurately represented position or a relevant alternative with a stated basis. Familiar debates do not establish the user's view; distinguish it from quoted positions and your own inferences.

Related statements do not form an inference merely by appearing together. A connective cannot repair a missing premise. When claims appear inconsistent, check whether different conditions explain them before treating them as a contradiction.

## Scope and uncertainty

Before deleting a qualifier, check whether it limits viewpoint, time, place, object, operating conditions, evidence, or claim strength. Fluency after deletion does not prove redundancy. Keep or reword limits whose removal broadens the claim. Combine repeated expressions of the same uncertainty while retaining distinct conditions and reasons.

Check what each quantity and qualifier attaches to. Moving a phrase can assign a property to a different entity even when every word remains. Subjects appearing together do not necessarily share a measurement or scope.

Interpret intensifiers in their domain: a defined technical meaning differs from general emphasis. Superiority or significance requires a relevant baseline and scope, not merely stronger wording.

## Context and agency

Judge a phrase by its claim, support, and function. Its pattern or grammatical subject alone does not establish a defect. Necessary contrasts, supplied reactions, and supported emphasis may remain within the entrypoint's rules.

Technical shorthand and biological function can describe behavior without implying conscious intention; a domain label alone establishes no motive. Recast misleading wording as the supported process, actor, or relationship without filling gaps with an invented actor or cause.

Use the supplied stance or creative brief to judge whether irony, reactions, or personification belong. Figurative writing does not establish a factual explanation.

## Reader interpretation

Use the audience's expected knowledge, the artifact, and references available to them. Check entities, terms, pronouns, sources, conditions, and actions. For names and comments, consider a maintainer who has not seen the task. Resolve missing context within scope without inserting private conversation history.

Taking a reader's perspective is an internal check. Independent reader testing requires a separate reader without that history or an intended verdict.
