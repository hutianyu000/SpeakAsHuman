# Reasoning

Read relevant sections when SKILL.md and the task rules leave questions about attribution, scope, intent, or what a reader can infer. This reference explains the shared constraints; it is not required for every expression task.

## Claims and attribution

Make clear which speaker, document, or body of evidence an attribution refers to, and represent what it supplies accurately. A claim in the input does not establish that its author conducted a study, collected statistics, or verified a result. Do not invent who gathered evidence, its source or method, or whether it was checked. Vague references to research, reports, or data cannot supply missing support. When discussing input material, identify it and distinguish its statements from independently checked results.

A rebuttal must address a position represented accurately or a relevant alternative whose basis is stated. A contrast used to correct a misunderstanding must express a real distinction. Familiar debates about the topic do not establish the user's position. Distinguish the user's stated view, quoted views, and your own inferences.

Check that each conclusion follows from its support and that definitions, premises, and conditions stay consistent. Related statements do not form an argument merely by appearing together. Do not repair a missing inference by adding a connective or silently assuming a premise. In long text, compare claims and scope across sections; retain distinct cases when their conditions explain an apparent difference.

## Scope and uncertainty

Before deleting a phrase that sets context or qualifies a claim, check whether it limits the viewpoint, time, place, object, operating conditions, evidence, or strength of the claim. The sentence may remain fluent after losing one of these limits. Keep or reword the phrase if deleting it would broaden the claim or increase certainty. Combine repeated expressions of the same uncertainty while retaining distinct conditions and reasons for uncertainty.

Check what each quantity and qualifier applies to. Moving a phrase can assign an area, limit, result, or property to a different entity even when the words remain. Two subjects appearing in the same sentence do not necessarily share a measurement or scope.

Identify the claim modified by an intensifier or comparison and judge its strength in the relevant domain. Keep defined technical meanings, supported comparisons, and the degree of uncertainty. Claims of superiority or significance need an appropriate baseline and scope. Stronger wording cannot supply missing support.

## Context and agency

When wording depends on context, identify its claim, its support, and what it helps the reader understand. A familiar phrase pattern or grammatical subject alone is not enough to judge it. Keep necessary contrast, supported emphasis, supplied reactions, and technical descriptions when they meet these conditions. Explicit prohibitions still apply.

Interpret roles defined in a domain and descriptions of biological function in context. Established technical shorthand may describe behavior or mechanisms supported by the material, but must not imply unsupported conscious intention or motive. A domain label alone does not establish intention. Reword misleading passages to describe the actor, process, or relationship the material supports. Do not invent an unknown actor or replace an unsupported motive with an unsupported cause.

Preserve the author's supplied stance or reaction within the task's boundaries. Do not add a personal stance, emotion, or contrarian pose to simulate a human voice. Figurative agency belongs to an authorized creative or figurative task and must not be passed off as a factual explanation.

## Reader interpretation

Read using only the knowledge reasonably expected of the audience, the text itself, and references made available to them. Check whether entities, terms, pronouns, sources, conditions, and requested actions are clear. For names and comments, consider a maintainer who has not seen the task. Supply missing context within scope without inserting the private conversation history.

Call this independent reader testing only if a separate reader assessed the text without that history. Reviewing your own text from a reader's perspective is an internal check.
