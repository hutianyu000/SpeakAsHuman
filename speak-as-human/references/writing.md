# Writing, Polishing, and Article Review

## Editing level and boundaries

Identify the purpose, audience, genre, and requested operation. Language polishing covers wording, grammar, local word order, and transitions. Content restructuring changes paragraph order, argument structure, selection of material, or arrangement of claims. Permission to polish does not automatically authorize restructuring or changes of position.

Use explicit style requirements, relevant writing samples, and stable habits in the source to establish the author's voice. Prefer available samples from the same document type, intended audience, publication venue, team, or repository when calibrating register, terminology, detail, and format. Assess which features fit the present purpose instead of transplanting the distinctive voice of an unrelated sample. Preserve supported stance, formality, terminology, cadence, and meaningful irregularities. Voice affects expression within the entrypoint's mandatory rules; it does not permit prohibited punctuation or habits, invented experience, or unsupported claims. Without a sample, infer only what the source and genre support rather than installing a stock personality. Calibration does not require external research or a fixed number of samples for every task.

For scholarly text, preserve study conditions, comparison baselines, numerical and statistical meaning, and citation scope. Keep observations, assumptions, hypotheses, inferences, and proved results distinguishable. Limit review conclusions to the passages and supporting material actually examined; language polishing alone does not verify scientific correctness.

## Propositions and attribution

Distinguish the author's claims, quoted positions, and the narrator's analysis. Paraphrases and quotations must preserve restrictions and attribution; do not present a position under discussion as a conclusion already accepted by the author.

Write the finished artifact for its intended readers. Keep references to the editing input, revision process, and verification limitations in separate editorial notes unless the requested genre discusses source documents or the user requests inline annotations. Preserve the original evidentiary status when polishing; do not turn supplied assertions into externally sourced findings by adding attribution. A missing citation alone does not authorize deleting or changing the claim. Identify material source gaps separately, without inventing citations, methods, or authority or filling the artifact with generic source labels.

In literature discussion, explain relevance and transferability through the studied objects, methods, findings, and limits. Keep editorial decisions about selecting, ranking, or allocating space to references in revision notes. Preserve substantive assessments of evidence quality and research relationships when they belong to the argument and have support.

## Reasoning and consistency

Match claims to evidence: a conclusion's scope must not exceed its support. Distinguish what examples, correlations, causal explanations, and proofs can establish.

Research motivation and contribution statements should identify the problem, relevant gap, resulting capability, or supported finding. Retain them when they establish purpose or position the work for its readers. Evaluate claims of novelty, importance, or superiority against the available sources and comparison scope; do not supply missing literature claims or replace their justification with praise.

Check definitions, conditions, and comparison baselines for consistency. If a title, opening, or paragraph promises an explanation, the body should provide it. Identify missing reasoning when a passage merely announces or repeats the conclusion.

Range and progression statements need a meaningful dimension and a supported relationship between their endpoints or stages. Do not imply coverage, continuity, or increasing maturity merely by joining different topics. State parallel subjects as parallel when the material establishes no ordered relationship.

For long argumentative text, compare the central claims, scope conditions, definitions, and baselines across sections. Distinguish parallel description from actual inference; require a valid link wherever the text claims causation, deduction, or explanation. Do not repair a logical gap by adding a connective or an unsupported premise.

## Literature synthesis

For reviews and related work, organize material around research questions, approaches, or documented developments when the task authorizes drafting or restructuring. Introduce the organizing relationship and use studies to support it. Use author order or chronology when attribution or historical development is itself relevant. Do not invent categories or stages, and do not treat polishing as authorization to reorganize the literature.

Allocate detail according to relevance to the section's question and the role each study actually plays. Explain central work's problem, contribution, and supported limits; describe adjacent work's specific connection without treating it as direct evidence for a different object. Establish these roles from the available material rather than assigning unsupported importance or precedence.

Synthesize what an approach resolves, its demonstrated scope, and remaining questions when the material supports that assessment. Retain individual studies where readers need their methods, results, or attribution. Avoid an author-by-author inventory that leaves the relationship unexplained, and avoid adding a formulaic assessment or summary to every paragraph.

Attach each citation or citation group to the specific claim it supports. Separate claims and groups when their evidence serves different functions; keep a larger group when its members support the same judgment. Place citations with the relevant clause or sentence according to the publication format, keeping attribution clear without a detached generic source label. Preserve citation keys and support relationships; do not remove references to meet an arbitrary group size or invent a mapping between sources and claims when the material is insufficient.

## Information and response

Organize the text around its purpose. Each paragraph should serve an identifiable function, such as posing a problem, presenting evidence, explaining a relationship, stating a qualification, or advancing a conclusion. Consider deleting or merging paragraphs when they serve no useful function.

Limit background to what the intended reader needs. Judge restatement by its reading position and function. Abstracts, section summaries, and conclusions may restate findings for readers entering at different points; consistent terminology and a necessary reminder of a condition may also require repetition. Retain useful synthesis and required conclusions. Delete or merge repetition when its function is already fulfilled and it adds no needed integration, qualification, or orientation. Do not remove material needed to understand the argument just to shorten the text.

Limitations and future directions should identify a boundary, unresolved question, or concrete research objective and its basis in the work. Preserve their prospective status. Remove generic forecasts or celebratory endings that contribute no such information, and qualify predictions whose certainty exceeds the support.

## Wording and structure

Arrange paragraphs and sections by their content relationships. Transitions should show how the topic or argument advances. Lists suit parallel information or procedures; paragraphs suit reasoning that needs development.

Inspect the whole text for recurring setup, reversal, emphasis, and closing patterns. A prohibited move remains prohibited when distributed across sentences or repeated at section scale. Remove empty run-ups and repeated conclusions that serve no remaining function under the information rules above. Preserve parallel structures when they express actual classifications, comparisons, or corresponding results; vary organization only when the information calls for it, without imposing artificial asymmetry or sentence-length alternation.

When word substitutions leave the same defect, rewrite the affected passage around the information it needs to convey. Preserve claims, relations, and protected content. If the repair requires broader restructuring than authorized, identify that limitation rather than perform cosmetic substitutions or silently widen the edit.

Enforce the entrypoint's mandatory prose rules before judging optional stylistic choices. Within those rules, check whether emphasis, degree modifiers, and rhetorical devices are justified and fit the genre. Retain compliant expression that serves explanation, emphasis, or artistic purpose. Adjust structure when it obstructs comprehension and modifiers when they mislead.

Inspect clauses appended to facts for claimed significance, impact, or contribution. Retain them when they add needed information or a supported relationship. Remove empty significance statements; flag substantive claims whose support is unresolved within the editing scope. Do not replace a vague statement with invented details, sources, or mechanisms.
