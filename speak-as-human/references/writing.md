# Writing, Polishing, and Article Review

## Editing level and boundaries

Identify the purpose, audience, genre, and requested operation. Language polishing covers wording, grammar, local word order, and transitions. Content restructuring changes paragraph order, argument structure, selection of material, or arrangement of claims. Permission to polish does not automatically authorize restructuring or changes of position.

Follow explicit style requirements and use relevant writing samples or recurring choices in the source to understand the author's voice. Prefer available samples from the same document type, audience, publication, team, or repository. Use them to judge formality, terminology, detail, and format, taking only features that fit the present task.

Preserve the author's position as established by the source, along with formality, terminology, rhythm, and irregularities that serve a purpose. Author style does not permit prohibited punctuation or habits, invented experiences, or unsupported claims. Without a sample, rely on the source and genre. Do not assign a generic personality to the author. No external research or fixed number of samples is required for this judgment.

For scholarly text, preserve study conditions, comparison baselines, numerical and statistical meaning, and citation scope. Keep observations, assumptions, hypotheses, inferences, and proved results distinguishable. Limit review conclusions to the passages and supporting material actually examined; language polishing alone does not verify scientific correctness.

## Propositions and attribution

Distinguish the author's claims, quoted positions, and the narrator's analysis. Paraphrases and quotations must preserve restrictions and attribution; do not present a position under discussion as a conclusion already accepted by the author.

Write the finished text for its intended readers. Put comments about the editing input, revision process, and limits of verification in separate editorial notes, unless the genre discusses source documents or the user requests inline annotations. Preserve how each claim is supported. Do not add attribution that turns a supplied assertion into a finding from an external source.

A missing citation alone does not authorize changing or deleting a claim. Report important source gaps separately. Do not invent citations, methods, or authority, or fill the text with vague source labels.

When discussing literature, use the objects studied, methods, findings, and limits to explain why the work is relevant and where its results may apply. Put editorial decisions about selecting, ranking, or allocating space to references in revision notes. Keep supported assessments of evidence quality and relationships between studies when they belong in the argument.

## Reasoning and consistency

Match claims to evidence: a conclusion's scope must not exceed its support. Distinguish what examples, correlations, causal explanations, and proofs can establish.

Research motivation and contribution statements should identify the problem, relevant gap, what the work enables, or a finding supported by its evidence. Retain statements that explain the purpose or place the work in context. Check claims of novelty, importance, or superiority against the available sources and the stated comparisons. Do not invent claims about prior work or substitute praise for justification.

Check definitions, conditions, and comparison baselines for consistency. If a title, opening, or paragraph promises an explanation, the body should provide it. Identify missing reasoning when a passage merely announces or repeats the conclusion.

When describing a range or progression, identify the dimension being compared and the relationship between its endpoints or stages. Joining topics does not establish coverage, continuity, or increasing maturity. Present topics as parallel when the material gives no basis for ordering them.

For long argumentative text, compare the central claims, scope conditions, definitions, and baselines across sections. Distinguish parallel description from actual inference; require a valid link wherever the text claims causation, deduction, or explanation. Do not repair a logical gap by adding a connective or an unsupported premise.

## Literature synthesis

For reviews and related work, organize material around research questions, approaches, or documented developments when the task authorizes drafting or restructuring. Introduce the organizing relationship and use studies to support it. Use author order or chronology when attribution or historical development is itself relevant. Do not invent categories or stages, and do not treat polishing as authorization to reorganize the literature.

Give each study space according to its relevance to the section's question and its role in the argument. Explain the problem, contribution, and established limits of central work. For related work, explain the specific connection; do not treat it as direct evidence about an object it did not study. Use the available material to establish these roles, without inventing importance or precedence.

Where the material allows, explain what an approach resolves, where it has been shown to work, and what remains unresolved. Retain individual studies when readers need their methods, results, or attribution. Explain the relationship between studies instead of merely listing authors. Do not append a formulaic assessment or summary to every paragraph.

Attach each citation or citation group to the specific claim it supports. Separate claims and groups when their evidence serves different functions; keep a larger group when its members support the same judgment. Place citations with the relevant clause or sentence according to the publication format, keeping attribution clear without a detached generic source label. Preserve citation keys and support relationships; do not remove references to meet an arbitrary group size or invent a mapping between sources and claims when the material is insufficient.

## Information and response

Organize the text around its purpose. Each paragraph should serve an identifiable function, such as posing a problem, presenting evidence, explaining a relationship, stating a qualification, or advancing a conclusion. Consider deleting or merging paragraphs when they serve no useful function.

Include the background the intended reader needs. Judge repetition by where it appears and what it does. Abstracts, section summaries, and conclusions may repeat findings for readers entering at different points. Consistent terminology and reminders of important conditions may also require repetition. Keep useful synthesis and required conclusions. Delete or merge repetition when it adds no needed connection, qualification, or guidance. Do not shorten the text at the cost of understanding the argument.

Limitations and future directions should identify a boundary, unresolved question, or concrete research objective grounded in the work. Keep plans and predictions distinct from completed work. Remove generic forecasts or celebratory endings that add none of this information, and qualify predictions that exceed the evidence.

## Wording and structure

Arrange paragraphs and sections by their content relationships. Transitions should show how the topic or argument advances. Lists suit parallel information or procedures; paragraphs suit reasoning that needs development.

Check the whole text for repeated setups, reversals, emphasis, and closing patterns. Prohibited rhetoric remains prohibited when spread across sentences or sections. Remove lead-ins and repeated conclusions that add nothing the reader needs. Keep parallel structures for actual classifications, comparisons, or corresponding results. Change the organization when the information calls for it, without forcing asymmetry or alternating sentence lengths.

If replacing words leaves the same defect, rewrite the passage around what it needs to convey. Preserve claims, relationships, and protected content. If the repair needs restructuring beyond the authorized scope, explain that limit. Do not hide the problem with cosmetic substitutions or silently broaden the edit.

Enforce the entrypoint's mandatory prose rules before judging optional stylistic choices. Within those rules, check whether emphasis, degree modifiers, and rhetorical devices are justified and fit the genre. Retain compliant expression that serves explanation, emphasis, or artistic purpose. Adjust structure when it obstructs comprehension and modifiers when they mislead.

Inspect clauses appended to facts for claimed significance, impact, or contribution. Retain them when they add needed information or a supported relationship. Remove empty significance statements; flag substantive claims whose support is unresolved within the editing scope. Do not replace a vague statement with invented details, sources, or mechanisms.
