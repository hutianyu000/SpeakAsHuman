# Writing, Polishing, and Article Review

## Editing level and boundaries

Identify the purpose, audience, genre, and requested operation. Language polishing covers wording, grammar, local word order, and transitions. Content restructuring changes paragraph order, argument structure, selection of material, or arrangement of claims. Permission to polish does not automatically authorize restructuring or changes of position.

Use explicit style requirements, relevant samples, and recurring source choices to understand the author's voice. Prefer available samples from the same document type, audience, publication, team, or repository. Use them to judge formality, terminology, detail, and format; adopt only features that fit the task and shared rules. Preserve meaningful rhythm and irregularities. Without samples, rely on the source and genre rather than assigning a generic personality. No external research or fixed sample count is required.

For scholarly text, preserve study conditions, comparison baselines, numerical and statistical meaning, and citation scope. Keep observations, assumptions, hypotheses, inferences, and proved results distinguishable. Limit review conclusions to the passages and supporting material actually examined; language polishing alone does not verify scientific correctness.

## Propositions and attribution

Distinguish the author's conclusions from positions merely quoted or examined. Consult reasoning.md when attribution or evidentiary status is unclear.

Write for the intended readers. Put comments about editing inputs, the revision process, and verification limits in separate editorial notes, unless the genre discusses source documents or the user requests inline annotations. A missing citation alone does not authorize changing or deleting a claim; report important source gaps separately.

When discussing literature, use the objects studied, methods, findings, and limits to explain why the work is relevant and where its results may apply. Put editorial decisions about selecting, ranking, or allocating space to references in revision notes. Keep supported assessments of evidence quality and relationships between studies when they belong in the argument.

## Reasoning and consistency

Research motivation and contribution statements should identify the problem, relevant gap, what the work enables, or a finding supported by its evidence. Retain useful context. Assess novelty, importance, and superiority against available sources and stated comparisons. If a title, opening, or paragraph promises an explanation, check whether the body supplies it rather than repeating the conclusion.

When describing a range or progression, identify the dimension being compared and the relationship between its endpoints or stages. Joining topics does not establish coverage, continuity, or increasing maturity. Present topics as parallel when the material gives no basis for ordering them.

For long arguments, compare central claims, scope conditions, definitions, and baselines across sections. Check which steps rely on examples, correlations, causal explanations, or proofs and what each establishes. Consult reasoning.md for disputed inference or apparently conflicting claims.

## Literature synthesis

When drafting or restructuring reviews and related work, organize material around research questions, approaches, or documented developments. Explain the organizing relationship. Author order or chronology fits when attribution or historical development is relevant. Categories and stages need support from the literature; polishing alone does not authorize reorganization.

Allocate space by each study's relevance and role. Explain the problem, contribution, and established limits of central work. For related work, state the specific connection; it is not direct evidence about an object it did not study.

Where the material allows, explain what an approach resolves, where it has been shown to work, and what remains unresolved. Retain individual studies when readers need their methods, results, or attribution. Explain the relationship between studies instead of merely listing authors. Do not append a formulaic assessment or summary to every paragraph.

Attach citations to the claims they support in the publication's format. Separate groups serving different evidentiary functions; keep a larger group when it supports the same judgment. Preserve citation keys and support relationships without imposing an arbitrary group size. If the source-to-claim mapping is unclear, report the gap rather than inventing it.

## Information and response

Organize the text around its purpose. Each paragraph should serve an identifiable function, such as posing a problem, presenting evidence, explaining a relationship, stating a qualification, or advancing a conclusion. Consider deleting or merging paragraphs when they serve no useful function.

Include needed background. Abstracts, section summaries, and conclusions may repeat findings for readers entering at different points; terminology and important conditions may also need repeating. Keep useful synthesis and required conclusions. Merge repetition only when it adds no needed connection, qualification, or guidance.

Limitations and future directions should identify a boundary, unresolved question, or concrete research objective grounded in the work. Keep plans and predictions distinct from completed work. Remove generic forecasts or celebratory endings that add none of this information, and qualify predictions that exceed the evidence.

## Wording and structure

Arrange paragraphs and sections by their content relationships. Transitions should show how the topic or argument advances. Lists suit parallel information or procedures; paragraphs suit reasoning that needs development.

Check article-scale setups, reversals, emphasis, and closing patterns. Remove lead-ins and repeated conclusions that add nothing needed. Parallel structure fits classifications, comparisons, and corresponding results; do not force asymmetry or alternating sentence lengths.

If substitutions leave the defect intact, rewrite around what the passage needs to convey within the authorized editing level. Explain when a repair requires broader restructuring. Retain permitted emphasis and artistic devices suited to the genre; change structure that obstructs comprehension and modifiers that mislead.

Inspect clauses appended to facts for claims of significance, impact, or contribution. Keep needed information and supported relationships, remove empty significance statements, and flag substantive claims whose support remains unresolved.
